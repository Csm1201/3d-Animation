# I Conducted My Own Russian Sleep Experiment (v4, built on the viral format)

**Format:** vertical 9:16 · about 2:05 · same toy 3D style · first-person voiceover from start to finish
**Built from:** `docs/research/format-analysis.md`. Its rules:
- The voice starts at frame 1.
- About 170 words a minute, with no dead air.
- A new problem every 10–15 seconds.
- Hour checkpoints and a finish line on screen.
- About 45 shots.
- Captions in the lower third with gold keywords.
- A callback punchline that loops.

**Title:** I Conducted My Own Russian Sleep Experiment 💤😳
**Voice:** AL narrates. Short acted lines from BRENDA, GARY and KEV.
**HUD (always on):** top-left, "HOURS AWAKE" with a big running number, a red bar underneath, and "FINISH LINE: HOUR 265".

Legend:
- **SHOT** = camera
- **SFX** = sound
- **CAP** = caption highlight in gold

### 0:00–0:06 · Hook: he just locked himself in by accident *(scene 1, already animated; add the voiceover)*
**SHOT:** side view. Al pounds on the chamber door, then turns to camera. The volunteers stare behind him.
**AL (VO):** "I just locked myself inside my own sleep experiment… by accident. And the three people in here with me have been awake for **seventy-two hours**."
**BRENDA (whisper):** "Welcome."
**SFX:** fist bangs, a door rattle, a needle scratch → freeze. **CAP:** LOCKED, BY ACCIDENT, 72 HOURS. **Text:** 72 HOURS EARLIER

### 0:06–0:18 · The myth
**SHOT:** snap to bird's-eye of the spotless lab, HOUR 0 → a close-up of a CLASSIFIED folder → a red stamp.
**AL:** "Seventy-two hours earlier, I was a genius. You've heard the Russian Sleep Experiment: five prisoners, a sleep gas, fifteen days, *monsters*. Totally fake. Someone posted it online in 2010. So I ran the real one."
**SFX:** creepy drone that cuts to a rubber STAMP: **MADE UP**. **CAP:** RUSSIAN SLEEP EXPERIMENT, FAKE.

### 0:18–0:32 · The setup and the rule
**SHOT:** a name card for each volunteer (each about 2 seconds), then a push-in on Gerald the coffee tank, then the HUD finish line slides in.
**AL:** "Three volunteers. Gary, who swears he doesn't need sleep. Brenda, a mom of four who hasn't slept since 2015. And Kev, who came for the free pizza. No gas, just Gerald, a coffee tank the size of a fridge, and a door that only opens from the outside, so they can't quit. One rule: nobody sleeps until hour **two sixty-five**, one hour past the world record."
**SHOT add:** a 1-second insert of Al proudly patting the heavy door.
**SFX:** a pop for each name card, a steam hiss from Gerald, a "ding" as the finish line lands. **CAP:** GARY, BRENDA, KEV, OUTSIDE ONLY, HOUR 265.

### 0:32–0:45 · Hour 17: legally drunk
**SHOT:** front 3/4 of a strip of floor tape. Gary drifts off it, Kev drifts off it, then a snap zoom on Brenda walking it backwards.
**AL:** "Hour seventeen. Science says this much time awake hits your brain like 0.05 blood alcohol, and by hour twenty-four it's 0.10, legally drunk. So I make them walk a straight line. Gary misses. Kev misses."
**BRENDA (walking it backwards, deadpan):** "Four kids."
**SFX:** a trombone slide on each miss, a crowd "ooh" for Brenda. **CAP:** HOUR 17, 0.05, LEGALLY DRUNK.

### 0:45–0:58 · Hour 41: microsleep
**SHOT:** bird's-eye, Kev mid-rant → his head drops (the beat already animated) → jolt → cut to Al at the window → Al's head drops.
**KEV:** "…and that's why pineapple belongs on pi—"
**AL:** "Microsleep. Your brain shuts off for a few seconds without asking, sometimes with your eyes still open. I laughed at him. Then my face hit the desk."
**SFX:** all audio muffles for one second, then a gasp sting. A desk *thunk* for Al. **CAP:** HOUR 41, MICROSLEEP, EYES OPEN.

### 0:58–1:12 · Hour 60: falling apart
**SHOT:** close-up of sticky notes on everything (MUG, ALSO MUG, STAPLER (FRIEND)) → Gary crying at a stapler → a snap zoom on Gary screaming at the glass.
**AL:** "Hour sixty. They forget so many words they label everything. Gary cries at a stapler because it looked lonely. I write 'irritable' on my clipboard…"
**GARY (pressed against the glass):** "I'M NOT IRRITABLE!"
**AL:** "…and the glass cracks."
**SFX:** sad violin for the stapler, a scream, glass *crack*. **CAP:** HOUR 60, IRRITABLE.

### 1:12–1:27 · Hour 72: the stare, and the accident
**SHOT:** chase cam down the gloomy corridor, lights flickering → all three lined up at the window, staring, not blinking → Al opens the door and steps in, clipboard in both hands → his elbow knocks the door → it swings shut behind him → a snap zoom on Al's face.
**AL:** "Hour seventy-two. The lights flicker, and all three of them stop moving, line up at my window, and stare. No blinking. No talking. So I went in to check on them, with my clipboard in both hands… and bumped the door with my elbow. *Click.* The door I built to only open from the outside."
**SFX:** music drops out, a hum, a light buzz, then a tiny, polite *click* (not a slam), then one beat of silence. **CAP:** HOUR 72, NO BLINKING, CLICK, OUTSIDE ONLY.

### 1:27–1:45 · Escape attempts
**SHOT:** quick montage, each shot about 2 seconds:
- Bird's-eye of mugs laid out to spell HELP. The camera rotates and it reads HELLO.
- Al wedged in the air vent, legs kicking.
- Al pacing and pep-talking, with a Randy Gardner card sliding in.

**AL:** "I spell HELP with forty mugs. From the camera it says HELLO. I try the air vent and only my head fits. So I tell them about Randy Gardner, a teenager who stayed awake two hundred and sixty-four hours in 1964, got forgetful and grumpy, then slept fourteen hours and woke up fine. They just stare."
**SFX:** a clink for each mug, a vent *bonk*, an inspiring swell that cuts dead on "they just stare". **CAP:** HELP, HELLO, 264 HOURS, 1964.

### 1:45–1:58 · The twist
**SHOT:** Brenda shuffles past Al → close-up of the door handle → reveal of the **PULL** plate → the door swings open → a snap zoom on Al's face.
**AL:** "Then Brenda walks past me…"
**BRENDA (pulling the door open):** "It says pull."
**AL:** "I installed it backwards. They've known since hour twenty. They stayed for the free coffee. And the staring?"
**BRENDA:** "We were asleep. Eyes open. You said that's a thing."
**SFX:** a creak, a comedic sting, Gerald's steam "pfff" like a laugh. **CAP:** PULL, HOUR 20, ASLEEP.

### 1:58–2:05 · Punchline and loop
**SHOT:** bird's-eye. All four collapse on the bed. The counter flips to HOURS ASLEEP and races to 14. Then a new volunteer walks in with a pizza, the door clicks shut, he *pushes* it. Hard cut back to frame 1.
**AL:** "So the real horror of the Russian Sleep Experiment isn't monsters. It's that after three days awake, you'll push a pull door for seven hours."
**SFX:** snoring chord, door click, then cut on the first bang of the opening. **CAP:** PUSH, PULL, 7 HOURS.

## Production notes
- **About 380 words of voiceover and dialogue**, which runs 2:05–2:15 at the researched pace (170–180 wpm). Read it fast, with no pauses except the microsleep silence and the twist beat.
- **About 45 shots**, mixing four cameras: side view (emotion), chase cam (walking and dread), bird's-eye (layouts, the mug gag, the ending) and close-ups (punchlines, the PULL plate).
- **Captions:** lower third, 2–5 words, popping in with the voice, white with a heavy black outline, gold on numbers, hours and punch words.
- **Scene 1 change:** add Al's voiceover from frame 1, and move the captions down to the lower third.
- **Loop:** the last frame (a new volunteer pushing the door) cuts straight into the first bang of scene 1.

## Fact sources
- Randy Gardner, 264 hours in 1964, then 14 hours of sleep; forgetfulness and irritability: [NPR, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274/sleep-deprivation-record)
- 17 hours awake ≈ 0.05% BAC, 24 hours ≈ 0.10%: [CDC / NIOSH](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/emres/longhourstraining/impaired.html)
- The Russian Sleep Experiment is a fictional internet story (about 2010): [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2022/01/fact-check-russian-sleep-experiment-was-not-real-event.html), [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/russian-sleep-experiment)

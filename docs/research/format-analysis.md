## The format, element by element

### 1. Hook: the first 2 seconds
- **The voice starts at frame 1, over action.** "I was walking home… when a car slammed into my body." "What if, starting right now, you could never die?" "They painted the jail cell pink…"
- **The first sentence holds the whole premise**, and usually a shock or a contradiction.
- **The on-screen text repeats the spoken words** as the first caption, so viewers with sound off are hooked too.
- No logo, no title card, no silent build-up.

### 2. Narration
- **Speed:** about 165–200 words per minute, with no dead air.
  - Retroyap: about 330 words in 2:00.
  - Snail: about 370 words in 2:03.
  - His Story: about 400 words in 2:01.
- **Chained sentences:** "…until one morning…", "So you…", "But then…". Each line hands off to the next problem.
- **Person:** first person ("I", His Story) or second person ("you", Retroyap) puts the viewer inside the story. Third-person retelling (DevRamo) works for a famous story told fast.
- **Acted dialogue** in quotes, with character voices (His Story). Short lines carry the emotion.
- **Concrete numbers and specifics** make it feel true: "8 tons", "11 feet an hour", "1 pint red + 1 gallon white".

### 3. Structure: escalation every 10–15 seconds
- **Problem → fix → the fix fails → a bigger problem** (Snail: tungsten box → rocket → star-hopping → cosmic loophole).
- **Time checkpoints on screen** (YEAR 1,000 → YEAR 1 BILLION) work as chapter markers.
- **A finish line** (FINISH LINE: 1 TRILLION YEARS) gives viewers a reason to stay to the end.
- **The emotional arc flips:** wish-fulfilment or cockiness → loss → a twist or bleak payoff.
- **A rule set up early, then broken** (Pink Cell's "exactly 15 minutes"). The broken rule is the climax.

### 4. Visuals and editing
- **Average shot about 1.5–3 seconds**, about 40–45 cuts per 2 minutes.
- **Snap zooms on punchlines and impacts.** Whip pans. Zoom-outs to show scale.
- **The main character stays centred** while the world changes around them.
- **A world-space label on the hero** (a "YOU" marker in the reference).

### 5. Captions
- **Lower third, centred**, 1–5 words at a time, popping in word by word in sync with the voice.
- **Bold, rounded sans-serif in white with a heavy black outline.**
- **Keywords, numbers and time labels highlighted in gold or orange.**

### 6. Sound
- **SFX on every action:** impacts, whooshes, ticking clocks, a needle scratch before reveals.
- **The music changes with the mood:** playful → tense → a drone or silence for the dark beat.

### 7. Ending
- **A punchline that calls back** to an earlier setup ("I dunk on the legs she paid for").
- **No spoken "subscribe" outro.** His Story hides SUBSCRIBE as a texture on the ball.
- **A loop:** the final line or frame flows into the first (the reference cuts to black, then to frame 1; DevRamo ends mid-word).

### 8. Titles
- **Short, emotional, curiosity-driven, often with an emoji:** "She Drove Past The Hospital😳", "Russian Sleep Experiment 💤😳", "I would never want to live forever, here's why".
- **The exact topic keyword in the title** when it's a known story.

## What this changes in our video
1. **Al speaks from frame 1** of the trapped scene, over the banging. Keep the scene and add the voice.
2. **The voiceover runs the whole video** (about 350 words in about 2:05), first person, with acted lines from the volunteers.
3. **Captions move to the lower third**, word by word, with gold highlights on numbers and hours.
4. **An HOURS counter with a finish line:** "FINISH LINE: HOUR 265" (one hour past Randy Gardner's real record).
5. **A new problem every 10–15 seconds**, with on-screen hour labels.
6. **About 45 shots**, alternating the side view (emotion), the chase cam (walking), bird's-eye (layouts, the mug gag) and close-ups (punchlines).
7. **SFX on every action.** Music goes from playful to tense to creepy to a comic sting.
8. **The ending loops back:** a new volunteer gets trapped, which leads straight into the opening line.

## 2. Everything they do to get views

### Video length
- **30–70 seconds.** Dr. Healthcare usually runs 45–70 s, TIS 15–60 s, Psych2Go 50–60 s, and the viral psychology posts 15–75 s.
- Short "vs" Shorts (22–24 s) and 13–30 s one-fact Shorts often do best, because they loop.

### Titles: short, literal, curiosity, one emoji
These literal patterns recur across the winners:
- "Why You Shouldn't ___?" (Dr. Healthcare, 26M)
- "How Does ___ Actually Work?" / "How ___ Actually ___"
- "What Happens If / When You ___"
- "Why ___ 😱 / 🤔"
- "[N] Signs You're Not ___, But ___" (Psych2Go)
- "X vs Y – Who Would Win" (TIS, 6–11M)
- "This ___ Left Him With ___" (Dr. Healthcare's newer case-story style)

3–7 words, plain language, with the word "Actually" for curiosity. No clickbait that the video doesn't pay off.

### Hook (first 2 seconds)
1. **The first spoken line is the title** (TIS, Rennrat, NarrArty, marvlunee), and it's on screen from frame 1.
2. **Show the situation or the object immediately.** The stomach's stir-stick snaps. Grandma pockets a rice ball on CCTV. There's no intro or logo.
3. **Make a counter-intuitive claim, or challenge a belief:** "Many parents believe…", "…and they *want* to get caught."

### Script
- **One idea per Short:** one causal chain, one study or one named concept, ending in a one-line takeaway.
- **The structures that work:**
  - Mini-sitcom: characters struggle → the human cause → the consequence escalates → an expert explains (Dr. Healthcare).
  - Study → test → result → name the effect (Rennrat, NarrArty).
  - "Imagine you're…" second-person story → social reveal → punchline (marvlunee).
  - Numbered list with a title card per point (Psych2Go).
- **Speed:** 130–190 words a minute (about 70–170 words per Short), with no filler.
- **Save the payoff for the end:** hold the number or reveal until the last line.

### Characters (the biggest differentiator)
- **Give things a personality.** Dr. Healthcare's organs have faces, glasses and voices: a nervous bone, a grumpy stomach. This is why a flat 2D health channel reached 1M subscribers in about 12 months.
- **Use multiple voices:** a squeaky character, a parent, a calm expert.
- **Show expressions with swapped faces:** sweat drops, tears, winks.

### Visuals and editing
- **Characters animated as rigged puppets** on flat backgrounds, cutaway diagrams and thought bubbles showing the mechanism.
- **Motion carries the video more than cuts.** Animated channels get away with a cut every 2–10 s if something is always moving. Faster explainers cut every 1.5–3 s with push-ins and snap zooms.
- **A/B split screens** work as cheap, high-performing formats (Noir Mind 4.4M, the "vs" Shorts).

### Captions
- 1–3 words at a time, popping in as they're spoken.
- Bold sans-serif in white with a thick black outline, at the bottom centre.
- Optional: numbers and keywords in yellow, danger words in red.

### Sound
- **A cartoon sound effect on every action:** pop, whoosh, gulp, snap, door chime.
- **Light music, mixed low:** playful, or soft piano for the emotional ones.
- **AI or human voice both reach millions,** so voice type doesn't decide views.

### Ending
- **A punchline or reversal, then an abrupt stop that loops back to the hook.** None of the top videos uses a spoken "subscribe" call to action.
- Alternatives: hide "SUBSCRIBE" on a prop, ask a comment question, or say "like for part 2" on a series.

### Upload cadence and series
- **Daily.** Dr. Healthcare posts about 30 a month, and TIS's Shorts peaked when it posted daily. SimpliHow stopped, and its views collapsed from 37M to 40–137K.
- **Series and sequels:** "Countries Impossible To Invade #3", "Part 1/2/3", "Explained in 60 seconds", reused winning titles.

### Avoid
- Race or political controversy bait (SimpliHow's 35M "Why Asian People…" drew 28K comments). It's high-risk and bad for the brand.
- Misleading health claims.

## 3b. Gap check (follow-up): "how businesses make money" is not open

A direct YouTube search on 2026-11-15 (vidIQ, sorted by views) shows this topic is **already flooded**:

**"How gyms make money" alone:** at least 13 animated or faceless channels made this exact Short between August and November 2026. Examples: Dollar Decoded, Wealth Progression, wait wat, CRAZY FINANCE, Money Socho, Sticky Financier, Wealth Theory, Ming Knows Why, The Friction Mind, Erty, How Businesses Really Work, Margintale and Hidden Margin. Every one has **12–1,477 views**, and many say "made with AI" in the description.

**Other business-model Shorts from 2025–26:** the same pattern. Costlore (Amazon), Dollar logic (insurance), Caveman Economics, EconWhys, Knox Tales (Costco) and Harsh Badhan (GoPro) are mostly brand-new channels with **10–1,500 views**.

**What does get big views in money:**
- "Starbucks is Actually a Bank" (Smart Nonsense, 14M, 2022): one surprising twist.
- "How Rich Thinks | 2d Animation" (Abhay Create, 24.8M): a character skit, not an explainer.
- "This Is How The Stock Market Works" (Luminate Mindset, 11.7M on a 54K-sub channel, 376×): a simple story.

**Verdict:** the *topic* (how businesses or money work) is a crowded gold rush where almost nobody wins. The viewers reward **characters, story and one big twist**, not a business breakdown. Whatever gap exists is in *execution*: a recurring cartoon cast and sitcom-style scenes, as Dr. Healthcare does with health. It is not in the topic.

## 4. Our plan: same format, different videos (revised by the gap check above)

**The niche: "City Money".** One guy walks down a modern street, and each Short explains the hidden money behind something he passes. Nobody in flat 2D owns this, demand is rising, and **the street we already built is the set.** Every shop on it is an episode.

**Our version of the winning format:**
- **Length:** 35–55 s, about 100–150 words at about 170 words a minute.
- **Hook:** the first spoken line is the title, over the guy walking past the shop, frame 1.
- **Characters (our version of Dr. Healthcare's organs):**
  - The street objects talk: a smug **$6 latte**, a gym **membership card** that loves people who never come, a nervous **wallet**.
  - The guy reacts, and a calm narrator explains.
- **Structure:**
  1. The object brags or schemes (comedy).
  2. The real numbers (cutaway diagram, yellow captions).
  3. The twist.
  4. A one-line takeaway, then an abrupt loop back to the walk.
- **Look:** the existing 2D street and guy, captions in our style, a sound effect on every action.
- **Cadence:** daily if possible. Numbered series ("Street Money #3").

# "Just Chillin'" (30-second clip)

**Format:** 1080×1920 vertical for TikTok/Shorts (plus a 1920×1080 cut), 30 fps, 900 frames, with audio
**Logline:** Two background NPCs treat a 5-star police chase that runs one of them over six times as a completely normal Tuesday, and the cops give the victim a ticket.
**Setting:** Sunset beach-city street. Pastel buildings, neon signs, palm trees, a bench, sidewalk above a two-lane road.
**Characters:**
- **Dave** (Pedestrian #4812): Hawaiian shirt, sunglasses, iced coffee. Tired, deadpan, swears casually.
- **Carol** (Pedestrian #0031): An old lady NPC who has stood in the exact same spot since launch because the studio never gave her a walk animation. Completely unbothered by anything.
- **Mike**: The police helicopter pilot. Knows Dave.
- **Officer Brandt**: A cop who takes paperwork very seriously.
- **The player**: A pink sports car with a 5-star wanted level, chased by five police cars and the helicopter.

**Comedy style:** Absurd things treated as completely normal. Everyone stays calm and matter-of-fact, the logic escalates until it's insane, swearing is casual rather than shouted, and it ends with a sincere lesson speech that gets undercut. The satire target is open-world games: the people who live in them have the worst lives in fiction, and nobody cares.
**Cartoon rule:** All hits are cartoon physics. Dave flattens like a pancake and pops back. No blood or injuries.
**Hook (0.0–2.0s):** Dave lying flat on the road while five police cars drive over him, one after another, and he says "ow" to each like he's checking boxes.

**Beat:** hook (run over 5×, calm "ow"s) → normal-day chat with absurd sad facts → chase arrives on schedule → his own AI betrays him → mid-air small talk with the pilot → run over again → the victim gets the ticket → sincere lesson speech → hit mid-speech → Carol closes it out.

**Why it works in this style:**
- Nobody reacts to anything. A car chase, a launch into the sky and five run-overs are treated like weather.
- The worst facts are said in the most casual way ("They never gave me a walking animation").
- The logic escalates: run over by the cops → ticketed by the same cops → second ticket for being bumpy.
- The swearing is casual and conversational ("That's fucked up, Carol"), not screamed.
- It has a point: a satire of how games treat the background people in them.
- The sincere speech sets up the final hit, which is a tradition of the genre.
